# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Octomind is for high school students who need to manage classes, homework, deadlines, sleep, and the rest of their week without turning planning into another chore. They use it in short planning windows before or after school, between assignments, or when new coursework appears and they need to know where it fits.

The primary job is to turn obligations into a usable schedule quickly enough that students keep trusting it. The unauthenticated surface should work like a marketing page: show why planning matters, demonstrate the scheduling experience, and make the value clear before login. The authenticated surface should behave like a practical calendar tool that helps students plan homework and tasks, remember what needs doing, and avoid running out of time.

## Product Purpose

Octomind helps students save time and reduce planning friction by turning school commitments, deadlines, sleep, and tasks into a schedule they can actually follow. Success means a student can add or import work, see when it should happen, adjust the plan, and leave with confidence that nothing important is being forgotten.

## Positioning

Octomind saves students time by making homework planning convenient: deadlines and tasks become a clear calendar plan around classes and sleep.

## Brand Personality

The product should feel calm, focused, and supportive, with enough brightness to feel appropriate for a student productivity app. The tone should be clear and encouraging without sounding childish, overly corporate, or like generic AI software. The brand's logo is an octopus because it has 8 hands that can help it manage all of its work. The brand should have some type of clean, water themed element to it (whether it be color scheme or symbols).

## Anti-references

Octomind should not feel like a generic AI SaaS dashboard. Avoid heavy enterprise-calendar clutter, vague AI automation claims, dense productivity systems that require setup work before value, and gamified interfaces that distract from getting schoolwork planned.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the schedule, not the system. Planning should feel immediate, visible, and low-effort.
2. Respect student time and energy. Every screen should reduce cognitive load and make the next action obvious.
3. Show convenience through concrete moments. Demonstrations, empty states, and onboarding should make it clear how tasks become calendar time.
4. Keep the product bright but steady. Use color to support attention, status, and student-friendly energy without making the app noisy.
5. Make trust visible. Deadlines, protected sleep, school hours, sync status, and errors should be explicit enough that users understand why the plan looks the way it does.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Design and implementation should target accessible web UI. Preserve keyboard-friendly controls, visible focus states, reduced-motion support, color-blind-safe status cues that do not rely on color alone, and readable contrast for body text, placeholders, controls, and status messages.
